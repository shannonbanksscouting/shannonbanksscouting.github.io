Activity Guide

Knots & Lashings

Three essential knots every Scout should learn

For younger sections (ages 6–8), we focus on simple, functional knots that build coordination and are genuinely useful. Here are three essential knots every young person should learn!

1

Reef Knot (Square Knot)

The most common "starter" knot. Used for joining two pieces of rope of equal thickness. It's the basis for tying bandages and parcels.

Uses:

  • Tying bandages
  • Wrapping parcels
  • Tying shoelaces (the flat part!)
  • Joining two ropes together
Remember: "Right over left, left over right, makes a knot both tidy and tight!"
2

Clove Hitch

A versatile knot used to secure a rope to a post or spar. It's the starting point for many pioneering projects.

Uses:

  • Tying a rope to a pole
  • Starting pioneering projects
  • Securing a tent line to a peg
  • Quick temporary fastening
Remember: Two loops that cross over each other around the pole!
3

Bowline (The King of Knots!)

Known as the "King of Knots" — the Bowline creates a fixed loop at the end of a rope. It won't slip or jam under load. Rescuers use this knot!

Uses:

  • Creating a rescue loop
  • Tying a boat to a dock
  • Making a loop that won't tighten
  • Securing climbing ropes
Remember the Story: "The rabbit comes out of the hole, goes around the tree, and back down the hole!"

Tips for Practising at Home

🪢

Use the Right Rope

A soft, flexible rope about 60cm long is perfect for practice.

🔁

Practice Makes Perfect

Try tying each knot 10 times in a row until your hands remember.

👀

Watch Then Try

Watch the video, pause it, then try yourself. Rewind if needed!

👨‍👩‍👧

Teach Someone Else

The best way to learn is to teach! Show a family member.
